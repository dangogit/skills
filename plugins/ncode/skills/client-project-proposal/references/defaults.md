# Your commercial and client-copy defaults

Fill in the `[bracket]` values once, before the first proposal. The policies below are the
recommended defaults; change any of them to match how you work. Apply them unless the current
request or the client's own template says otherwise. They are internal: none of this file's
reasoning goes into client copy.

- Contact email: `[email]`
- Logo file: `[path to a PNG with a transparent background, or none]`

- Where finished proposals are saved: `[folder]`

## Pricing

- Day rate: `[your day rate]` plus VAT per active workday. Estimate bottom-up in workdays for the
  way you actually work, then multiply. The rate and the workday count stay internal.
- Client copy shows one total price for the whole system, plus VAT and the total with VAT. No
  price per stage or milestone, and no workday counts anywhere, so the day rate cannot be inferred.

## Payment and run-in

- Two equal payments: 50% before work starts, with approval of the proposal, and 50% on delivery.
  Define delivery in the document as a concrete, visible outcome (for example "the whole team
  works from the system").
- 30 days of run-in after delivery are included in the price: bug fixes and small adjustments.
  Maintenance and improvements after that are a separate monthly retainer, in a separate proposal,
  with no figure in this one.

## Schedule

- Show each stage with a date range, calculated from an assumed start date stated in the document
  (usually the first working day after the validity date). Add a Gantt-style chart by week.
- State that a later start moves every date by the same gap, and that time spent waiting for the
  client or a third party (approvals, access, answers) moves the dates, not the price.

## What client copy never says

- Never mention the AI coding tools you build with, and do not justify the price or speed by them.
  AI that is a feature of the delivered product may be described as a feature.
- No internal estimate reasoning, reserve allocation, day counts or rate.
- No unverified third-party prices or approval times. Name the provider and say who pays it.
